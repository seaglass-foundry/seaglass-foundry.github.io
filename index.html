ForgeGIS™ 1.2.6 vs GDAL 3.12

Checked against GDAL. And faster.

15×
geometric-mean speedup over GDAL
across 242 benchmark operations at sizes from 16×16 to 8192×8192 pixels; warm timings on a laptop RTX 5070
6×
kernel against kernel
GDAL in process, on operations where both sides take at least 1 ms; 256×256 to 4096×4096 and real elevation tiles
  • Parity: 1191 checks pass, 0 fail. 884 of them compare ForgeGIS’s output with GDAL’s or an independent oracle, within a tolerance stated per operation; the rest are consistency or smoke checks.
  • 3 of the 242 were slower than GDAL, all tiny: raster creation and rasterize at 256×256, and raster creation at 1024×1024.

Performance demonstration · not the benchmark

One take, real time: operations 1–9 of a 61-operation race, each run once on each side (1:26). Watch the full uncut race.

Method. Benchmark of record 20261004-094534-191f1b44: ForgeGIS 1.2.6 against GDAL 3.12.1 in process (3.11.5 for command-line pipelines), NVIDIA RTX 5070 Laptop GPU, Windows 11. Timing protocol v4: warm runs, the median of each block, the GPU kept in its working state between timed blocks. The geometric mean covers every GDAL-referenced operation with both sides timed, no category excluded; sub-millisecond rows are included, which is why the in-process figure for work of 1 ms or more sits beside it. The timing method was tightened in this release, so earlier published figures are not directly comparable. The race above is a separate demonstration, not the benchmark. A precision WGS-84 virtual globe in double precision, and the suite’s optional 3D view. Measurements name their geodesic method, MIL-STD-2525C symbols draw with their proper frames, and a scenario plays, pauses and steps on a timeline.
